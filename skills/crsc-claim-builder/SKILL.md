# CRSC Claim Builder

Helps a military retiree file a Combat-Related Special Compensation (CRSC) claim or reconsideration that a service CRSC board can approve, and estimates what it will pay. The governing rules are DoD 7000.14-R, Volume 7B, Chapter 63 (the FMR chapter), cited below as "FMR." The board decides each VA diagnostic code separately, on documents, by a preponderance of "credible, objective documentary information" (FMR 10.1.1). Quality beats quantity.

This is a preparation aid, not legal or financial advice. Free accredited help is available from VSOs and NVLSP.

## Workflow

1. **Intake.** Collect documents before writing anything. Read every one; do not rely on the veteran's summary.
2. **Eligibility and money.** Run the calculator (below). Tell the veteran the monthly ceiling, the rating that reaches it, and which conditions matter. This decides where effort goes.
3. **Evidence map.** For each candidate condition, find the event, the combat-related criterion, and the earliest in-service record tying the condition to that event.
4. **Draft.** One DD 2860 disability page per VA code, plus a short cover memo. For a denial, add a DD 3210.
5. **Check.** Re-read every sentence against the records. Remove anything the records contradict.

## 1. Intake checklist

Ask for these, in this order of value:

- **VA rating decision (full narrative, every page)** plus the benefit summary letter. Titles, codes, percentages and effective dates must be copied from it word for word. The "Reasons for Decision" paragraph for each code shows whether VA granted it directly (38 CFR 3.303/3.304) or as secondary (3.310). Only 3.310 grants count as secondaries on the 2860.
- **Retirement order** and **DD 214.** Look for the 26 U.S.C. 104 lines ("combat related injury ... YES", "instrumentality of war ... YES") and the years-of-service figures (10 USC 1208 for active, 10 USC 12733 for Guard/Reserve).
- **PEB findings (DA 199 / DAF 356 / NAVPERS equivalent)** if medically retired. Column E codes each condition: A (armed conflict), I (instrumentality of war), S (simulating war), H (hazardous service). A PEB combat code is strong evidence but not binding (FMR 5.2 note).
- **Retiree Account Statement** (gross retired pay, VA waiver amount, SBP).
- **Event records:** orders placing the member at the event, training records, mishap/safety reports, LOD (AF 348/DA 2173), Purple Heart citation, flight records, deployment orders, award citations.
- **Service treatment records** from the months after the event, especially flight physicals, PHAs and sick call notes.
- **MEB narrative summary**, VA C&P exams (DBQs).
- **Denial letter**, if any. Its stated reason is the target.

## 2. Combat-related criteria (FMR 6.0)

| Code | FMR | Test | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC Armed conflict | 6.1 | Definite causal link to hostile action; being in a war zone is not enough | Combat orders, CAR/CIB/CAB, Purple Heart, incident reports |
| HS Hazardous service | 6.2 | Includes but is not limited to aerial flight, parachute, demolition, experimental stress, diving; injury must be the direct result of performing that duty | Flight records, jump logs, dive logs, flight surgeon notes |
| SW Simulating war | 6.3 | Combat training such as exercises, live fire, obstacle courses, combatives; the list is illustrative ("such as"); excludes routine PT and sports | Training orders, course records, incident notes |
| IN Instrumentality of war | 6.4 | Direct causal relationship to a vehicle, vessel or device designed primarily for military service, or a civilian item used in a way peculiar to military service | Records naming the equipment and the mechanism |
| PH Purple Heart | 5.1 | Disability attributable to the PH injury | Citation plus medical records of what was injured |

The August 2026 DD 2860 lists four codes in item 14.h and adds a presumptive-condition question at 14.c (for example burn pit or Agent Orange). Plead the strongest code on the form and the others "in the alternative" in the narrative.

## 3. Writing rules that win

- **One discrete event per condition.** "Cumulative exposure over 20 years" reads as ordinary occupational wear and is the most common denial. Name the date, place, mechanism and record.
- **Cite the earliest in-service record.** A note written for treatment years before any claim outweighs a later statement. Quote it exactly.
- **Quote the VA and PEB in their own words** when they name the event ("your harness was not secure"). That beats a veteran's own retelling recorded in a clinic history.
- **Never assert something the records contradict.** Before writing "no prior history," search the earlier records. A false absolute sentence costs more than silence.
- **Choose what to attach.** The applicant must produce documents in their possession that support the claim (FMR 10.1.2); unrelated history that only confuses the issue does not need to be attached. Never alter a record or omit a relevant part of an attached record.
- **Secondaries:** list a secondary only when VA's decision says "secondary to" or cites 3.310. Otherwise it needs its own page.
- **Match the 2860 title to the VA decision word for word.** One condition per page.
- **Unit and location blocks** must match the orders for the event date.
- **SMC:** ask for a combat-related SMC determination (FMR 7.2) only when the underlying code is also claimed and SMC would raise the payment above the ceiling.
- **Dates:** if records disagree on the event date, reconcile them in one paragraph. Weather, PCS, deployment or evacuation gaps explain delayed treatment; say so plainly.

## 4. Forms and filing

- **First claim:** DD 2860 (Aug 2026 edition). Page 1 asks for DoD ID and, at item 10, occupational specialty. One page 2 per condition. Narrative is item 14.k; put "SEE ATTACHED CONTINUATION, SHEET X" there and attach a typed page.
- **After a denial:** DD 3210 "CRSC Reconsideration Request" (Aug 2026), signed in ink or CAC. Check the "providing the requested information" box and list the VA codes. Send only new and substantive documentation, plus a revised DD 2860 if the original sheets were wrong. A cover memo that answers the denial reason point by point helps.
- **Copies only, never originals.** Certified mail with tracking.

## 5. Money

### The rules

- **Gross CRSC (FMR 8.1):** the VA rate for the combined rating of only the approved combat-related codes, **including dependents** (8.1.1), plus combat-related SMC (8.1.2). Combine with the VA table (FMR 9.2, 38 CFR 4.25 Table I): highest rating first, each step's combined value rounded to a whole number, then the final value rounded to the nearest 10 with 5 rounding up.
- **Waiver limit (FMR 8.2):** CRSC cannot exceed the retired pay currently waived for VA compensation.
- **Chapter 61 retirees with under 20 years (FMR 8.5.2.2):** CRSC plus the retired pay left after the VA offset cannot exceed 2.5% x years x retired pay base. Use 10 USC 1208 years for active component and 10 USC 12733 years for Guard/Reserve (shown on the retirement order). Under the Blended Retirement System use 2.0%.
- **Chapter 61 with 20+ years (FMR 8.5.2.1):** CRSC plus remaining retired pay cannot exceed the longevity retired pay the member would otherwise receive.
- **Longevity retirees:** no Chapter 61 reduction; CRSC is limited only by the waiver.
- **Retired pay base:** if the retiree statement does not show it, divide gross retired pay by the multiplier used: the larger of the disability percentage or 2.5% x years, capped at 75%. The ceiling grows with each retired pay COLA (8.5.3).
- **CRSC vs CRDP:** a retiree eligible for both must pick one each January (FMR 3.2). CRDP needs 50%+ and 20 years.

### Rating needed to max

Find the smallest VA rating tier whose rate (with the member's dependents) meets the ceiling. Then list the smallest sets of conditions that combine to that tier. Effort goes to those conditions first; the rest are insurance.

### Backpay

- **Soto v. United States (June 2025):** the six-year Barring Act limit does not apply to CRSC.
- **DoD interim guidance (20 Aug 2025) and clarification (30 Jan 2026)** tied the effective date to the date the service received the application. **DoD withdrew those limits on 14 May 2026**; the effective date is the first month the member was retired, receiving VA compensation with retired pay waived, and had a qualifying combat-related disability, no earlier than the statutory floors (Jun 2003, Jan 2004, Jan 2008 for Chapter 61 under 20 years).
- Policy has changed three times since 2025 and litigation continues (Ploe v. U.S.). Check current guidance, and in every claim or reconsideration ask in writing for the statutory effective date.
- Estimate = months from effective date to approval x monthly CRSC, with months before the rate table's effective date at the prior year's rates (2.8% lower for the December 2025 table). Also show the smaller figure under the receipt-date rule so the veteran sees the risk.
- SBP premiums may be taken from CRSC when retired pay is too small to cover them (FMR 8.3).

### Calculator

The calculator is bundled with this skill at `scripts/crsc_calc.py`, a path relative to the folder that holds this SKILL.md. In Claude Code the folder is `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/crsc-claim-builder`. It needs Python 3.9+ and only the standard library. It reads local files and makes no network calls.

1. Write the veteran's numbers to a JSON file shaped like `examples/example_input.json`.
2. Run `python3 scripts/crsc_calc.py input.json`.
3. Read `warnings` first. If the VA rate table is more than a year old, tell the veteran the figures use last year's rates.

Input fields:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `retirement_type` | `longevity`, `chapter61_under20` or `chapter61_20plus` |
| `years_for_cap` | 10 USC 1208 (active) or 12733 (Guard/Reserve) years from the retirement order. Required for Chapter 61. |
| `retired_pay_base`, `gross_retired_pay`, `va_waiver` | Monthly figures from the Retiree Account Statement |
| `brs` | `true` for Blended Retirement System (2.0% per year, max 60%). Default is 2.5% per year, max 75%. |
| `multiplier_override` | Total multiplier, such as `0.4375`, when the order states one. Overrides `brs` and `years_for_cap`. |
| `cola_factor` | Retired pay COLAs since retirement, such as `1.028`. Default `1.0`. |
| `dependents` | `spouse`, `children_under_18`, `children_in_school`, `parents`, `spouse_aid_attendance` |
| `combat_related_smc` | Monthly combat-related SMC, if any |
| `conditions` | `name`, `code`, `pct` (0-100 in steps of 10), and `combat`: `true` (strong), `"maybe"` (claimable) or `false`. Only `true` and `"maybe"` are combined. |
| `backpay` | `effective_date`, `through_date`, optional `receipt_date`, optional `monthly_amount` |

Ratings combine by 38 CFR 4.25 Table I: each step rounds to a whole number before the next, so 60, 30 and 10 give 75, which rounds to 80. VA rates load from the newest `data/va_rates_*.json`. Each December the maintainer adds a new file from va.gov with its `effective` date and the COLA percentage. Months before that date are paid at the prior year's rates in the backpay estimate.

The script exits with `error: ...` on bad input. Fix the field it names and run it again. Do not guess a missing figure. Ask the veteran for the document that shows it.

Report back in plain language:
1. Maximum monthly CRSC and which limit sets it (waiver or Chapter 61 cap).
2. The VA rating tier that reaches it, and the smallest condition sets that get there.
3. What each condition is worth alone.
4. Backpay under the statutory effective date, and under the receipt-date rule.
5. The caveat that DFAS computes the actual amount.

## 6. Output

Give the veteran:
- A one-page summary: money table, which conditions to fight for, and the denial reason answered.
- DD 2860 entries per condition (title, code, dates, percentages, combat code, unit, location, secondaries) and a typed 14.k continuation for each.
- A cover memo: request, what is new, the event, the records, the FMR criteria met, effective date request.
- An attachment list with exact page numbers, and a short list of records deliberately not attached with the reason.

## Data handling

The veteran's records stay in this conversation. Do not send them to any outside service, do not save them anywhere the veteran did not ask for, and keep the calculator input to the numbers and condition names it needs. Leave Social Security numbers and DoD IDs out of drafts; the veteran fills those in on the signed form. Never put SSNs, DoD IDs or medical details into anything meant for a public group.
